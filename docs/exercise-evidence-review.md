# CourtReady exercise-library evidence review

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-24

**Scope:** 111 catalog drills, their category-level coaching notes and doses, and the workout engine's screening / session-generation rules.
**Important limit:** this is a desk review of general guidance, not a clinical evaluation of each movement, a medical screen, or validation of CourtReady's generated programs. A qualified coach or clinician should adapt training to an athlete's maturity, skill, health, current symptoms, equipment, and total practice/game load.

## What the sources support

- **Youth resistance training:** NSCA's position statement and the AAP clinical report support properly designed, age-appropriate, supervised youth strength training. Both emphasize instruction, technique, and gradual progression. They do not justify treating a generated plan as safe without appropriate adult oversight.
- **Plyometrics:** NSCA's coaching article emphasizes movement mastery, quality over quantity, limited exercise variety, recovery, and accounting for the jumps already completed in sport. CourtReady now selects at most two drills from its explicit plyometric category per workout; other jumps in warm-ups or power blocks and basketball practice still count toward the athlete's total.
- **Basketball warm-ups:** the SHRed Injuries Basketball study reported a lower ankle/knee injury rate with its specific 10-minute neuromuscular program in youth teams (IRR 0.64, 95% CI 0.51–0.79). It was a quasi-experimental team-season study. CourtReady's warm-up is not SHRed and is not claimed to reproduce its effect.
- **Stretching:** the 2025 expert Delphi consensus distinguishes flexibility work from preparation for explosive performance. The library uses dynamic movement in warm-ups and identifies longer static holds as flexibility/recovery work rather than a required pre-jump step. Stretching is not presented as an injury treatment.
- **Workload:** IOC guidance stresses the athlete's context and exposure to both training and competition; it does not support using one number as an injury forecast. Session-RPE multiplied by duration is a practical estimate of internal load, not a diagnosis. CourtReady workload/readiness outputs should be treated as prompts for reflection, not safety guarantees.

## Changes made during the review

1. Removed BMI-based impact caps and the BMI-derived "body composition"/impact-management labels. BMI does not measure movement skill, tissue capacity, or landing readiness; profile weight remains available for the app's separate nutrition calculations.
2. Removed blanket adult impact limits based only on ages 40 and 50. Adult eligibility now depends on readiness, injury flags, and proximity to games. Existing conservative under-16 impact bands and a few drill-specific minimum-age filters remain product heuristics, not clinical cutoffs; the app now states this and recommends qualified supervision for youth.
3. Capped the number of separately selected plyometric drills in one generated session at two, and the warm-up selector will not stack multiple higher-impact drill variants. This is a volume guardrail, not a guarantee that total weekly jump load is appropriate.
4. Changed the game-day session intensity label from "peak" to "maintain" so game-day preparation is not represented as maximal training. Advanced strength/power cues now stop short of failure and emphasize technique.
5. Replaced a fixed pre-workout water-plus-salt instruction with individualized, non-forced hydration language. Softened unsupported injury-prevention and recovery claims, removed a safety guarantee from injury substitutions, and revised high-pull/power wording to emphasize controllable technique.
6. Recent-load readiness now prefers the athlete's completed-session RPE × duration when available, following the general session-RPE method; planned load remains only as a fallback. The app's scaling and penalty values remain heuristics, not validated thresholds.
7. Added category-level citations and scope notes to each drill card, plus original animated stick-figure sketches. The animations are schematic visual references, not exact technique demonstrations.

## Limits still visible in the product

- The dose progressions across all 111 drills have not each been validated in a controlled study. They remain coaching starting points, and individual drills may need to be changed or omitted.
- Age bands, readiness cutoffs, game proximity rules, injury flags, and profile-based selection are software heuristics. They cannot assess biological maturity, movement quality, medical history, pain cause, or whether an athlete is cleared to return to sport.
- The SHRed result applies to that program and the studied youth teams; it must not be attributed to CourtReady.
- Skill reps are basketball practice suggestions; sources do not establish the listed counts as optimal.
- GIF loops simplify multi-plane movement and do not show coaching context, common errors, speed, or equipment setup. Use a coach or qualified professional for loaded, high-impact, or unfamiliar movements.
